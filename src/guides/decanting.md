Rough rules, not laws: taste as you go. Drinking windows: [consumption windows](/guides/consumption-windows).

## Why {#why}

- **Sediment.** Old reds and vintage Port drop pigment and tannin. Decanting leaves the deposit in the bottle, so it does not make the wine gritty or bitter.
- **Air.** A young, closed wine can soften and show more aroma. An hour does not change the tannins much; it mostly lets off stuffy or reduced smells.
- **Not a cure.** Air cannot fix a flawed wine, and very old wines can fade quickly once exposed to it.

## What {#what}

| Wine | Decant? | Time before serving |
|---|---|---|
| Young, tannic reds (Cabernet, Syrah, Barolo, Ribera del Duero, Priorat) | Yes | 1-2 h; very closed ones up to 3 h |
| Medium reds (Rioja Crianza and Reserva, Mencía, Garnacha) | Optional | 30-60 min |
| Pinot Noir and red Burgundy | Only if old or unfiltered | Up to 30 min; fragile |
| Mature reds, 15+ years (Gran Reserva, old Bordeaux) | Yes, for sediment | Just before serving, about 30 min at most |
| Vintage Port | Yes, for sediment | About 30 min; finish it the same day |
| Full-bodied or old whites (white Burgundy, white Rioja Reserva) | Optional | About 30 min, kept cool |
| Light whites and rosé | No | |
| Champagne and sparkling | No | |
| Fino and manzanilla | No | Best fresh and cold |

## How {#how}

1. Stand the bottle upright for 24 hours, or at least a few hours, so the sediment settles.
2. Open it and wipe the neck clean.
3. Hold a light (a candle or your phone torch) under the neck while you pour: it lets you see the sediment coming up the neck.
4. Pour slowly and steadily in one go, more gently for old wines. Stop when the sediment reaches the neck.
5. Young wine in a hurry: swirl the decanter gently for about 30 seconds.
6. Taste now and then, and serve when it is ready. Most reds are best within 12-18 hours of decanting.

No decanter? A clean jug does the job. Simply opening the bottle does very little: the neck is too narrow.

Sources: Wine Folly, Wine Spectator, Decanter.
