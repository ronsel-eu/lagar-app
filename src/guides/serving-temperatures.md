title: Serving temperatures
summary: How cold to serve each kind of wine, and how to get there.

---

Rough ranges, not laws: a few degrees either way is a matter of taste. Cold hides aroma and sweetness, warmth shows alcohol, so adjust to the glass.

## By style {#styles}

| Wine | °C |
|---|---|
| Sparkling (cava, Champagne) and light, crisp whites | 3-7 |
| Rosé and full-bodied whites (oaked, white Rioja, white Burgundy) | 7-12 |
| Light and medium reds (Pinot Noir, Mencía, young Garnacha, Crianza) | 12-15 |
| Bold reds (Ribera del Duero, Priorat, Reserva, Barolo) | 15-18 |
| Sweet wines (Sauternes, late harvest, Moscatel) | 6-10 |

Fine sparkling and old whites show more if you let them warm a little in the glass.

## Sherry {#sherry}

| Style | °C |
|---|---|
| Fino and manzanilla | 7-9 |
| Amontillado, palo cortado and oloroso | 12-14 |
| Pedro Ximénez and cream | 12-14, or over ice |

## Getting there {#how}

- A cellar at 12-14 °C is already right for most reds and full whites.
- Whites and rosé: straight from the fridge are often too cold, so take them out 15-20 minutes before serving.
- Reds: a room-temperature red is usually too warm. About 15 minutes in the fridge, or 5 minutes in an ice bucket, brings it back.
- Sparkling: 2-3 hours in the fridge, or 20-30 minutes in ice and water.
- A wine that smells mostly of alcohol is too warm. One that tastes flat or mute is too cold.

Sources: Wine Folly, Catavino.
